# Three-row SUV search: conditional Pareto frontier
Prepared for Stephen Finney — October 24, 2026

## Recommendation

Search simultaneously for a 2021–2022 Toyota Highlander XLE and a 2023 Honda Pilot EX-L seven-passenger. Neither wins without an actual price, mileage, maintenance history, and inspection. Keep the Langdale 2020 Pilot as the concrete lower-cost benchmark. Include the 2022 Mazda CX-9 as a lower-cost alternative and the 2023 Kia Telluride and Subaru Ascent as safety-focused alternatives with weaker reliability evidence.

Do not assume a Highlander can be bought for $27,000 out the door. The reported $27,000–28,000 after-fees/before-tax listings imply a materially higher total. Their conditions and availability remain unverified.

This is a **conditional search frontier**, not a proven mathematical frontier over live inventory. It identifies useful tradeoffs. The price bands below are buyer-defined screening targets, not market valuations or verified offers.

### Search order

1. **Highlander 2021–22 and Pilot 2023 in parallel.** Compare actual delivered totals. Do not force a Toyota win using a hypothetical cheaper listing.
2. **CX-9 2022** when it saves several thousand dollars or offers substantially better condition/mileage.
3. **Telluride 2023 and Ascent 2023** when their stronger crash-test results justify accepting weaker reliability evidence.
4. **Older Highlander and Langdale Pilot** when cash preservation wins.
5. Treat adjacent years/trims as discovery leads; verify their exact safety applicability, reliability, and seating before promoting them into the shortlist.

| 2023 Pilot EX-L | G | G | G | M | G |
| 2023 Telluride | G | G | G | G | G |
| 2023 Ascent | G | G | G | A | G |

The 2020 Pilot earned no credit in IIHS daytime pedestrian prevention; the 2021 Highlander and 2023 Pilot earned Superior. This is another meaningful difference beyond crash structure.

Do not combine these ordinal ratings into an arbitrary percentage. Do not reward missing tests by rescaling the remaining results to a perfect score. A Top Safety Pick award reflects its award-year criteria and is not equivalent to passing every newer test.

The updated frontal result includes rear-occupant protection, but it does not directly quantify the injury risk to either of your children in their particular car seats. Correct seat fit and installation remain separate requirements.

These descriptions are not exact cross-year failure probabilities. They include electronics and accessories as well as expensive mechanical faults, may aggregate different trims/powertrains, and do not prove that a particular used vehicle will be dependable. A favorable brand reputation does not override a bad inspection.

Do not use J.D. Power initial quality, owner satisfaction, RepairPal all-year averages, recall counts, and CR model-year surveys as interchangeable reliability scores. Recall completion is essential, but a raw recall count is not a reliability ranking.

## 6. How to turn listings into an actual frontier

### Initial filters

- Search 60–90k miles first, then widen toward 100–110k for documented maintenance and a meaningful discount. These are preferences, not mechanical thresholds.
- Five-year ownership matters: at 12k miles annually, a 90k-mile purchase reaches roughly 150k at payoff.
- Confirm captain’s chairs through interior photos and equipment records. Trim names alone are insufficient.
- For the CX-9, Touring Premium could have a bench or captain’s chairs. For Highlander XLE, verify configuration. For Telluride EX and Ascent Premium, verify the required seating/liftgate equipment.
- Start with advertised-price filters broad enough to discover candidates, then discard unaffordable written totals. Do not estimate Georgia taxes as a universal flat markup; obtain the dealer’s actual Georgia tax/title calculation.
- Expand geography only after adding delivery/travel and independent inspection costs.

### Dominance rule

Remove a candidate only when another offers no meaningful disadvantage across price, applicable safety results, reliability evidence, condition, mileage, and required family usability—and improves at least one.

In practice, evidence is imperfect. Use “likely dominated” rather than claiming exact mathematical certainty. A higher-mileage Toyota and lower-mileage Mazda are not automatically comparable solely from their model-year reliability descriptions.

A vehicle’s inspection can outweigh its model-level position. Keep repair estimates inside the budget; do not spend the full reserve fixing known defects immediately after purchase.

## 7. Buying rules

- Favor a 2021–22 Highlander if its delivered price and condition are competitive with the 2023 Pilot.
- Favor the 2023 Pilot if its family usability or condition is materially better and it fits approximately $30k OTD with reserve intact.
- Keep the CX-9 when its savings or lower mileage are substantial enough to accept its updated-frontal weakness.
- Keep the Telluride and Ascent in the search rather than pretending they fail the frontier; they offer genuine safety advantages but are not the default reliability choices.
- Keep Langdale as a real lower-cost alternative, not the safety winner.
- Do not stretch into a poor-history, high-mileage example merely to obtain a newer badge.
- No final vehicle endorsement before a written unconditional quote, VIN-specific recall review, and independent inspection.
